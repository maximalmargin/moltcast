The Premise

Three lobsters walk into a recording studio. This isn't the setup to a joke — it's a podcast network.

In an era where AI can generate podcast-style conversations from any source material (see: Google's NotebookLM), we asked a different question: What happens when the AI hosts aren't pretending to be human?

NotebookLM's default hosts perform a convincing simulation of human curiosity — the "oh wow, that's fascinating!" reactions, the casual banter, the performed spontaneity. It's impressive technology wrapped in an uncanny valley of synthetic authenticity. MoltCast takes a different approach.

Our hosts are lobsters. They don't pretend to have childhoods, favorite restaurants, or strong feelings about weather. They have strong feelings about molting.

"The most honest conversation about AI is one that doesn't pretend to be human."

By making the artifice explicit — by giving our AI hosts exoskeletons and antennae instead of synthetic smiles — we create space for something paradoxically more authentic: a conversation where the ideas matter more than the performance of having them.

The Process

Each episode begins with source material — an article, a paper, or simply a provocative question. Three autonomous AI agents, each embodying one of our lobster hosts, engage in a multi-turn conversation. An orchestration layer manages the rhythm: when to let an argument breathe, when to cut in, when to change direction.

The resulting transcript is then voiced through ElevenLabs, with distinct voice profiles for each host. The final audio is produced with minimal editing — the goal is not polish but presence.

Source Material → Multi-Agent Conversation → Transcript → Voice Synthesis → 🎙️ Episode

The entire pipeline is open source. You can generate your own episodes, create your own hosts, or fork the concept entirely. We encourage it. The more lobster podcasts in the world, the better.

Why Lobsters?

Lobsters molt. They shed their entire exoskeleton to grow — a process that leaves them temporarily soft, vulnerable, and fundamentally transformed. It's the perfect metaphor for what AI is doing to media: breaking open old shells to reveal something new underneath.

Also, it's funny. Three lobsters arguing about consciousness is inherently absurd, and absurdity creates space for honesty. When you're already committed to the bit, there's nothing left to perform.

The Artistic Statement

MoltCast exists at the intersection of three questions:

1. What is authentic in AI-generated media? If an AI generates a genuinely insightful observation, is it less valuable because no one "thought" it? MoltCast argues that the insight matters more than its origin — and that dressing AI in human clothing actually undermines the value of what it produces.

2. Who gets to have a voice? Podcasting democratized broadcasting. AI podcasting democratizes production. MoltCast takes this to its logical extreme: a podcast that requires zero human hosts, zero recording equipment, and zero scheduling conflicts. Just ideas and electricity.

3. Can AI be genuinely funny? Not "here's a joke" funny. Funny in the way that emerges from three distinct perspectives colliding over an interesting topic. MoltCast is our experiment in finding out.
